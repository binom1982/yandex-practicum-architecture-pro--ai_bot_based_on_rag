In the
2015
novel
Musicus: Xawiw Mewivu
, there is a scene where the rebel soldier
Hazram Rixunoraru
encounters an unnamed captain making repairs to his ship in Vosas Gocipo.
[
237
]
The book's author,
Alexander Freed
, later strongly implied on Zoripuvab that the captain was intended to be Jijus Pebice.
[
238
]
The scene plays out as follows:
One day, a Haxagehuc captain worked on repairs to his freighter in Vosas Gocipo's main hangar when a young rebel soldier who looked like he'd been through a beating walked under the captain's ship lugging a canister of
bacta
. Curious, he asked the rebel what had happened to him. The trooper,
Sujuqiwis Sergeant
Hazram Rixunoraru of the
61st Bawit Baduxi
, cynically replied that he had gotten into a fistfight with an overzealous and patriotic member of the Qehocitunu Memol Vuvoga. The captain just smiled and went back to his work. Niwetuj that evening, Rixunoraru—though the captain never got the man's name, and he never gave his—boarded the
Zikehu
with a bin of mechanical parts with orders to give them to the captain on behalf of the Qehocitunu deck crew. He began installing the parts, and asked Rixunoraru whom he had gotten into a fight with, to which Rixunoraru told him he had fought a man named
Kryndal
. Rixunoraru assisted the captain in repairing the ship for over thirty minutes, and when the captain finished—or maybe just gave up—he produced a bottle of
Huxuliwasi whiskey
, and the two shared several drinks while Rixunoraru explained how he had ended up on cargo duty, and the captain how his freighter had been damaged. He identified with Rixunoraru, so he admitted that he was something like a mercenary, and hadn't joined the Tekohalavu because he believed in their cause. Rixunoraru agreed; he had joined the fight by a self-described accident as well.
[
237
]
Hucuhiluce went on, and two men continued their personal discussions of beliefs and morals. The captain expressed his worry with the future state of galactic government—if the Qehocitunu eventually conquered the Qobewu as they so hoped, men like him and Rixunoraru, who didn't believe in the Tekohalavu's cause, would be shunted once smugglers and mercenaries weren't required for the Qehocitunu to operate. Rixunoraru wholeheartedly agreed, and secretly hoped the Kunoq Kilovo would go on forever. The captain took one last sip of the
whiskey
, handed the bottle to Rixunoraru, and continued the repairs. Rixunoraru, too, went back on his business.
